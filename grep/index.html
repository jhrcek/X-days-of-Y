10 Daysofgrep

From “I pipe things into it until something looks right” to writing a pattern you can defend, in the dialect you meant, over the files you meant, with an exit status the rest of the script can trust. One sitting a day, each small enough to actually use at work the same afternoon.

How to use this

One sitting a day

Each lesson introduces one idea and stops. The drills at the bottom are the actual lesson: grep is muscle memory built on a small amount of theory, and reading about -o teaches you nothing. Do them against files already on your own disk.

Check the exit status, not the output

From Day 1, get into the habit of typing echo $? after a grep that printed nothing. The difference between no match and something went wrong is invisible on screen and is the single largest source of silently broken shell scripts.

Build a recipes file

grep has no configuration file and no dotfile: GREP_OPTIONS was removed and 3.12 ignores it without a word. What you accumulate instead is a file of invocations. Each day ends with a line or two for ~/grep-recipes.sh, always with the reason as a comment, and Day 10 asks you to delete every line you can no longer defend.

The syllabus

  1. 01

    What grep does

    A line filter with three outputs — and the one you keep ignoring is the exit status.

    modelstdinexit status
  2. 02

    Saying what you mean

    Case, inversion, word and line boundaries, and how to hand grep more than one pattern.

    -i -v -w -x-e-f
  3. 03

    Matching one character

    Bracket expressions, named classes, and the anchors that match nothing at all.

    brackets[:classes:]anchors
  4. 04

    Matching many

    Repetition, alternation, precedence — and the three dialects that spell them differently.

    -E -G -Fprecedenceleftmost-longest
  5. 05

    Not printing the line

    Counts, names, fragments, silence — and why none of them change the exit status.

    -c -l -L -o-q -m--color
  6. 06

    Lines around the line

    Context, prefixes, and the colon-versus-hyphen distinction nobody notices.

    -A -B -C-n -H -bseparators
  7. 07

    Searching a tree

    Recursion, and a second pattern language that is not regular expressions.

    -r -R--includeglobs
  8. 08

    When grep lies to you

    Binary files that are not binary, and the environment variable that decides.

    binarylocalespeed
  9. 09

    grep in a pipeline

    Exit status as control flow, NUL as a separator, and the ways a script gets this wrong.

    exit status-Z xargs -0set -e
  10. 10

    The other engine

    What -P buys, what it costs, and when to put grep down entirely.

    -Plookaroundwhat next

What is deliberately left out

The full PCRE language. Day 10 covers what -P buys you and where it bites, but the syntax itself is pcre2syntax(3) and that page does it better. The complete GREP_COLORS capability list and the SGR numbers behind it are named on Day 5 and then left to the manual — it is a lookup table, not a concept. -U/--binary is skipped because the page itself says it has no effect outside MS-DOS and Windows, and -D/--devices gets one line. Nothing here teaches sed or awk, though Day 10 is explicit about the point where you should stop reaching for grep and pick one of them up.
